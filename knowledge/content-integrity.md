# Content integrity

A durable guardrail, not a scaffold — sourced 2026-08-16 from a real editing session, where a caption
card attributed a specific quote/endorsement to a real, named public figure (a startup accelerator's
president), with nothing establishing that person actually said or endorsed it.

## Rule

**Never fabricate or extend a quote, endorsement, or attributed claim tied to a real, named person**
— in hooks, captions, on-screen cards, or IG post copy — without verifiable evidence they actually said
or endorsed it. This applies across every content-generating capability (`hook-generator`,
`reel-script-writer`, `caption-writer`, `video-editor`), not just video editing specifically.

## What's fine

- Referencing a real public figure discussing a *real, verifiable* topic they've actually spoken about
  publicly (e.g. citing a real podcast appearance) — this is fine, and already used as a legitimate
  pattern (see `content-pillars.md`'s GTM Engineering example, which cites a real regional business
  figure on a named podcast).
- Attributing something to a generic/unnamed role ("YC's president") without a quote-style attribution
  card, if the underlying claim itself is accurate.

## What's not fine

- A quote-style attribution card naming a specific real person, paired with words they haven't been shown
  to have actually said — even if the surrounding claim is directionally true.

## How to handle it in practice

If a piece of content hits this line, don't quietly drop it or quietly proceed — say so explicitly, name
the specific problem, and offer to continue with everything else in the piece while flagging exactly what
needs to change (drop the attribution, swap to a generic framing, or provide an actual verified source)
before that specific element gets built or polished further.
